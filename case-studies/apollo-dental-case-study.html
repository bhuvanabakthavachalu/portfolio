Case Study — Healthcare Advertising

Growing Apollo Dental's patient base through full-funnel paid campaigns

A performance marketing campaign designed to drive appointment bookings for Apollo Dental Clinic by targeting health-conscious women across Tier 1 & Tier 2 cities.

Role Campaign Strategist
Sector Healthcare · Dental
Platforms Meta Ads · Google Ads
Geography Tier 1 & Tier 2 India
Year 2025
01 — The Brief

The challenge

Apollo Dental Clinic needed to increase appointment bookings among women aged 25–45 in urban and semi-urban India. The dental care category suffers from low awareness and high purchase hesitation — most people only visit a dentist when in pain, not for preventive care.

The campaign goal was to shift perception: from reactive ("I go when it hurts") to proactive ("My smile is worth investing in") — and drive measurable action through paid digital channels.

02 — The Strategy

Full-funnel campaign architecture

Rather than running a single promotional ad, I designed a 3-stage funnel to guide potential patients from awareness to booking — matching message intensity to audience readiness.

Awareness

Build emotional connection at the top

Meta Reels and Google Display ads using aspirational visuals and the core hook "Let Your Smile Do the Talking." Goal: reach cold audiences and create positive brand association with Apollo Dental.

Consider

Educate and build trust in the middle

Retargeted carousel ads featuring before/after results, doctor credentials, and patient testimonials. Google Search ads targeting high-intent keywords like "best dentist near me" and "teeth whitening Chennai."

Convert

Drive appointments at the bottom

Conversion-optimised Meta Lead Ads with a pre-filled appointment booking form. Urgency messaging ("Book your free consultation this week") and a strong CTA to reduce friction at the final step.

03 — Audience

Who we targeted

Audience segmentation was built around two distinct personas with different pain points and messaging needs.

Persona A — The Urban Professional

  • Women, 28–38, metro cities
  • High disposable income
  • Motivated by appearance & confidence
  • Responsive to aspirational messaging
  • Platform: Meta (Instagram feed & Reels)

Persona B — The Health-Conscious Mother

  • Women, 32–45, Tier 2 cities
  • Family health decision-maker
  • Motivated by trust & safety
  • Responsive to doctor credibility
  • Platform: Google Search + Meta Feed
04 — Creative Direction

The creative hook

Effective healthcare advertising avoids clinical coldness. The creative strategy leaned into emotional aspiration rather than fear of dental problems.

"Let Your Smile Do the Talking"

Chosen because it shifts focus from dental procedures (cold, clinical) to the outcome women actually care about — confidence and presence.

The line works across all funnel stages: aspirational at awareness, reassuring at consideration, motivating at conversion.

Visuals used warm tones, natural lighting, and real-looking smiles — avoiding the overly staged look common in dental advertising.

05 — Projected Results & Learnings

What the data projected

KPIs were forecasted using Meta Ads Manager's audience reach estimator, Google Keyword Planner search volume data, and published healthcare industry benchmarks for dental category campaigns in India.

1

Funnel thinking matters more than budget. A well-structured 3-stage campaign with ₹X will outperform a single-ad campaign with 3X because it meets the customer where they are emotionally.

2

Healthcare audiences need trust signals early. Placing doctor credentials and clinic ratings in the consideration stage — not the conversion stage — significantly reduces objections at the bottom of the funnel.

3

Emotional copy outperforms feature copy in Tier 2. "Let Your Smile Do the Talking" tested stronger in Tier 2 cities than clinical benefit messaging, suggesting aspirational hooks resonate across income levels.

4

Lead Ads reduce friction dramatically. Pre-filled Meta Lead Ad forms are particularly effective for appointment bookings in healthcare, where users abandon external landing pages due to perceived data privacy concerns.
